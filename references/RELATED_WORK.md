# Related work and comparison of resources

The result studied here concerns uniform reconstruction of every local coefficient function of a repeatable, noncommuting, time-dependent Hamiltonian on a fixed-dimensional lattice. The waveform degree, local strength and observation horizon enter the resource bounds. Coherent replay and accurately compiled controls are explicit experimental resources; the output is a classical list of coefficient functions. See [the model](../science/model.md) and [the resource account](../science/resources.md) for the precise statement.

Several ingredients have established precedents: coherent accumulation in waveform sensing, adaptive cancellation of a learned Hamiltonian, repeated learned-inverse echoes, local randomized measurements, and high-precision lattice simulation. The contribution analyzed in this repository is their combination with a constructive local inverse for the noncommutative waveform response and a system-size-independent local remainder bound. These two steps make it possible to refine extensive local coefficient data without first obtaining an accurate global description of the propagator.

All comparisons below use the identified source versions. Bibliographic keys refer to [references.bib](references.bib). A claim about a particular theorem should be read with its access model and error metric.

For J2026, writing $`S`$ for the number of probe settings, $`N_s`$ for trajectories per setting, $`\sigma`$ for the minimum local inverse singular value and $`B_{\rm det}`$ for its deterministic bias, Theorem 1 requires

```math
N_s\geq
\frac{8Sn}{\Delta t^2}
\frac{\log(2Sn/\rho)}
{(\epsilon\sigma-\sqrt n B_{\rm det})^2},
\qquad \epsilon\sigma>\sqrt n B_{\rm det}.
```

This is a conditional bound. The explicit $`n`$ factor accompanies global $`\ell_2`$ error; a maximum-local-error target requires a different conversion. It does not establish a size advantage for our different norm. Sections refer to the [author-posted full text dated 20 July 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/410594334_Rigorous_Time-dependent_Hamiltonian_Learning_via_Continuous_Weak_Measurements).

P2017's growing-horizon frequency model and this repository's fixed-horizon coefficient model have different parameter generators. A frequency derivative can grow with elapsed time, whereas the static amplitude subfamily used for our lower bound has a uniformly bounded parameter derivative. Its $`T^4`$ Fisher information therefore does not conflict with the inverse-accuracy lower bound stated here.

Earlier experiments also reconstructed noncommuting single-qubit time-dependent Hamiltonians; see de Clercq et al., [Nature Communications 7, 11218 (2016)](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms11218). Thus full time resolution and noncommutativity are themselves established goals. The issue addressed here is their combination with extensive local output and a complete coherent precision-refinement bound.

It is not a dimension-independent extensive waveform guarantee. The version matters: this comparison uses v2, dated 29 September 2026. |

The local argument here avoids imposing a small global operator norm on the extensive residual. Finite-time dressing gives an interaction with bounded local strength and a bounded first support-size moment. The resulting double-commutator estimate controls each measured one-site observable under repeated echoes. Temporal recovery then uses

```math
R(t)=L'_t+i[\widehat H(t),L_t],
```

so only a bounded set of additional local Pauli coefficients is needed for each original interaction. This is the point at which a local Hamiltonian statement is obtained from echo data; the existence of a generic unitary bootstrap alone does not perform that conversion.

## 3. Established mathematical and algorithmic tools

- <a id="h2020"></a>**Local randomized measurements.** Huang, Kueng and Preskill, **H2020**, [arXiv:2002.08953v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.08953v2), main performance theorem and Supplementary Sections 1.B and 5.C, establish simultaneous prediction of many local observables from classical shadows. For a weight-$`w`$ Pauli observable the random-Pauli second-moment factor is $`3^w`$; arbitrary local observables require the corresponding shadow-norm bound. Our input/output process estimator is derived separately, with second moment at most $`3^{|B|+1}`$.
- <a id="nsy2019"></a>**Quasi-locality.** Nachtergaele, Sims and Young, **NSY2019**, [arXiv:1810.02428v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.02428v2), §3.1, §4, Lemma 5.1 and §5.4, develop time-dependent Lieb–Robinson bounds, conditional-expectation approximations, shell decompositions and transformed interactions. These supply the locality framework. The particular weighted interaction sums and repeated-echo double-commutator estimate used here are stated explicitly in [the local-response argument](../science/local_response.md).
- <a id="hhkl2020"></a>**Paid control simulation.** Haah, Hastings, Kothari and Low, **HHKL2020**, [arXiv:1801.03922v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.03922v4), Theorem 1 and §2, give near-linear spacetime gate cost with logarithmic precision overhead for efficiently specified, piecewise slowly varying lattice Hamiltonians. Applying the theorem to a known polynomial schedule requires charging coefficient evaluation, subdivision and requested simulation accuracy. The citation key identifies the 2020 preprint revision; it is not a claim that the journal publication occurred in 2020.
- <a id="trefethen2013"></a>**Stable temporal approximation.** Trefethen, **Trefethen2013**, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice*, Chapter 15, treats Lebesgue constants for interpolation. Chapter 3, Exercise 3.9, explicitly states Markov's polynomial derivative inequality; it appears in the [author's original-edition excerpt](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/ATAP/ATAPfirst6chapters.pdf). After rescaling to $`[0,T]`$, $`\|p'\|_\infty\leq 2m^2\|p\|_\infty/T`$. These are classical conditioning tools, rather than quantum improvements.

## 4. Fair experimental comparisons

**Coherent duration, samples and gates are separate resources.** A refinement stage uses a fixed accuracy for its local transfer estimates, but the number of coherent echo repetitions grows as the current coefficient error decreases. Fresh preparations, total unknown exposure, maximum coherent circuit duration, trusted gates, classical preprocessing and output size should all be reported. Parallel experiments reduce wall time without reducing aggregate unknown exposure.

**A fixed temporal grid does not mean fixed clock accuracy.** The finite-difference spacing can remain independent of $`\epsilon`$ at fixed degree. Prefix timing errors, waveform drift and coherent controller errors still accumulate during replay. The allowable error per echo decreases with the target accuracy, with the degree-dependent reconstruction factors included. The known inverse also acts on the same physical register and may entangle its initially product state. A device must supply that calibrated control capability while the unknown drive is switchable.

**Noise changes the attainable asymptotic regime.** The theorem assumes coherent unitary replay. General Markovian-noise limits are analyzed by Demkowicz-Dobrzański, Czajkowski and Sekatski, <a id="dcs2017"></a>**DCS2017**, [arXiv:1704.06280v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06280v3). Zhou et al., <a id="z2018"></a>**Z2018**, [arXiv:1706.02445v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.02445v2), Theorem 1, give the Hamiltonian-not-in-Lindblad-span condition for Heisenberg scaling with fast accurate controls and noiseless ancillas. For a single $`Z`$ signal with parallel $`Z`$ dephasing this condition fails. Accurate digital control by itself does not remove noise on an unknown analog signal.

**The lower bound concerns the precision exponent.** A static one-Pauli subfamily already requires $`\Omega(1/\epsilon)`$ aggregate unknown exposure for constant-probability uniform coefficient recovery. The hybrid argument in [resources](../science/resources.md) permits parameter-independent adaptive controls. It does not establish optimal dependence on degree, number of coefficients, control gates or laboratory wall time.

**Classical processing and quantum access should be matched.** F2026 already has polynomial classical postprocessing, so its comparison concerns acquisition resources. Classical fitting, direct integration and tensor-network propagation can be effective in regimes where their representations remain tractable. This repository does not give a universal computational separation from those methods, or a noise-independent practical crossover. Its claim is an experimental precision law under the stated coherent access and paid implementation model.
